# Analysis Contract

## The seven-layer chain

Use this order for every macro or asset question:

1. **Object and horizon**: define the variable, window, currency, and investable expression.
2. **State**: describe level, momentum/acceleration, and surprise versus expectations.
3. **Mechanism**: explain the causal chain and the policy reaction function.
4. **Pricing**: identify consensus, valuation, positioning, and what is already reflected.
5. **Scenarios**: keep a base, alternative, and tail scenario; assign conditional probabilities.
6. **Asset mapping**: estimate direction, relative strength, lag, asymmetry, and trend confirmation by asset.
7. **Falsification and review**: state what would change the view and how the forecast will be scored.

## Four distinct claims

Keep these separate in notes:

- **Fact**: observable in the stated information set.
- **Inference**: interpretation of facts through a mechanism.
- **Hypothesis**: a conditional relationship not yet established for the current regime.
- **Decision**: an action or stance chosen under uncertainty.

## Dominant market logic

Do not declare a narrative dominant merely because it is plausible. Look for at least two of:

- asset reactions to new data or policy surprises;
- changes in cross-asset correlation or volatility;
- whether good news still produces positive price response;
- consistent moves in rates, FX, equities, commodities, and credit;
- evidence that the marginal buyer/seller is responding to the same variable.

When evidence conflicts, record the conflict instead of forcing a single story.

## Conditional probability

Distinguish `P(scenario)` from `P(asset outcome | scenario)`:

`P(asset outcome) = sum(P(scenario) × P(asset outcome | scenario))`.

Probabilities must refer to observable events and fixed windows. Avoid precision that cannot be scored.

## Evidence roles

Classify every important indicator or source as one of:

- driver evidence;
- transmission evidence;
- market-pricing evidence;
- confirmation evidence;
- disconfirming evidence;
- noise or unresolved evidence.

Record whether the evidence is a level, change, surprise, or price signal. Do not count correlated indicators as independent proofs.

## Asset-specific cautions

- China style baskets overlap; describe their exposures before assigning a view.
- Overseas country indices combine global beta, sector mix, domestic factors, valuation, and FX.
- Gold is not a one-factor inflation hedge; separate real yields, USD, fiscal/monetary credibility, central-bank demand, positioning, and tail-risk demand.
- Liquidity is not one number; distinguish central-bank liquidity, bank credit, market funding, global USD liquidity, cross-border flows, and risk appetite.
- Policy transmission is a chain from objective → instrument → intermediary response → private behavior → real variable → earnings/discount rate → asset price, with lags and bottlenecks.

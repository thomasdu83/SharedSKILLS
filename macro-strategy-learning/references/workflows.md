# Workflows

## Weekly forecast

1. Locate the prior snapshot and confirm the information cutoff. If none exists, create a first snapshot and mark the missing baseline.
2. Record only new facts, expectation surprises, policy changes, and meaningful price responses.
3. Update growth, inflation, liquidity/credit, earnings, and policy states using level, momentum, surprise, and confidence.
4. Name the top one to three marginal drivers and write the causal chain.
5. Write what the market has priced and what remains unpriced; check trend, breadth, volatility, and cross-asset confirmation.
6. Create three scenarios with probabilities totaling 100%, triggers, conditional asset responses, and falsifiers.
7. Complete the China style and overseas country mapping using `references/asset-taxonomy.md`.
8. State stance, confidence, reasons for not being more aggressive/defensive, and next checks.
9. Freeze the note. Set the monthly anchor flag only on the chosen scoring snapshot. Do not overwrite after freezing.

## Maturity review

1. Open the original snapshot before looking at the realized path.
2. Reconstruct the evaluation window using information available at the time; label revised data.
3. Compare macro state, policy response, pricing, asset direction, relative strength, and timing.
4. Classify the error: macro, policy/transmission, pricing, mapping, horizon, decision, luck, or bad luck.
5. Score process quality separately from outcome quality. Compare with persistence, simple trend, and available consensus baselines.
6. Check probability calibration; do not treat overlapping weekly windows as independent observations.
7. Write transferable lessons and framework-change candidates. Do not directly rewrite the framework from one result.
8. Leave the note at `stage: synthesized` until human review is complete.

## Framework module

1. Define the object, boundary, horizon, currency, and asset expression.
2. Separate identities/institutional facts from empirical relationships and working hypotheses.
3. Draw the mechanism from driver to intermediary to expectation, pricing, and asset result.
4. Add state dependence, lags, bottlenecks, competing explanations, and failure conditions.
5. Map indicators to a mechanism and label each as level, momentum, surprise, or pricing evidence.
6. Add three scenarios, asset conditional responses, historical support, and counterexamples.
7. Link evidence cards and cases; record open questions and version changes.
8. Publish as a draft framework. Apply it in future weekly notes without editing past snapshots.

## Evidence and case

- **Indicator/claim**: use `TPL-Macro-Evidence-Card`; capture definition, causal position, data vintage, limits, support, contradiction, and current maturity.
- **Historical episode**: use `TPL-Macro-Historical-Case`; reconstruct the information set, consensus, shock, policy response, asset sequence, and counterfactual without hindsight.
- **Source material**: summarize only the reusable mechanism, evidence, or counterexample. Link the original source; do not turn every reading note into a permanent framework.

## System audit

Audit only after several cycles. Check template burden, missing fields, duplicate indicators, stale frameworks, unresolved questions, baseline comparisons, and whether the process changes behavior. Do not expand the framework merely because a single forecast failed.
